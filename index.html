Village History & Overview

Welcome To Mednichowki

In Mednichowki, there lived a kind woman named Medania. She had no children of her own, so all the children of the settlement went to Medania's house to play—and the village was named in her honor.

Originally, the administrative district of Mednichowki was Munger district, and it changed to Lakhisarai district during the year 1995–1996. Within Mednichowki are a bustling Market, the sacred Ganga River, a Primary School, a Mosque, a Madrasa, and a 150-year-old temple named Chandisthan, alongside the temple of Mata Durga built in 1952 and several other temples.

The people of Mednichowki are actively engaged in agriculture. Local farmers produce large quantities of pulses for both export and daily use, alongside wheat and menthol (mint) plants. Because the village has only a Primary School and no English-medium high schools or colleges for higher studies, students travel to the nearby village of Amarpur for secondary schooling and higher education.

🏪 Historic Market Pioneers (60–70+ Years of Trade)

  • First Grocery Shop: Opened by Akbar Khan and Rahman Khan, as recounted by village elders.
  • First Sweet Shop: Established in Mednichowki Market by Muso Saw and Baudhu Saw.
Historical Pioneer

R.K.P. Verma

Ram Kishun Prasad Verma

First Mukhiya of Mednichowki after the Gram Panchayati Raj Act (Khawba Japani Gram Panchayat, from Kiranpur to Amarpur). He served continuously for 39 years until 1998.

Farming & Rural Economy (agi.html)

Agriculture in Mednichowki

The majority of Mednichowki's population depends on farming—cultivating cash crops like Menthol alongside export-grade pulses, wheat, gram, and sweet peas.

High-Income Cash Crop

The Menthol Plant Revolution

The cultivation of the Menthol Plant is a transformative addition to this area. As recounted by the people of Mednichowki, the first farming of the Menthol Plant was pioneered by Zila Parishad Amit Sagar. After witnessing the strong economic benefits and higher income from menthol oil extraction, more and more local farmers adopted menthol cultivation.

Large landholders cultivate crops for commercial export, while smallholder farmers grow staples for household consumption and selling in the local Mednichowki market.

🌱 Sep/Oct – Feb/Mar

Gram (Chana)

Farming starts in late September or the beginning of October, and the crop matures for harvest by February–March.

🌾 Nov – Mar/Apr

Wheat (Gehun)

Sowing begins in November, and farmers harvest the golden wheat fields at the end of March and beginning of April.

🫘 Export Staple

Pulses (Arhar & Masoor)

Pulses including Arhar (Pigeon Pea) and Masoor (Lentils) are grown in heavy quantities for export and daily use.

🫛 Signature Taste

Peas (Matar)

Among all vegetables, Peas (Matar) are cultivated in the largest volume in Mednichowki and are prized for their special sweet taste.
